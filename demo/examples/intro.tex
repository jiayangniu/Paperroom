\section*{Introduction}
Comparing search methods under a fixed evaluation budget requires two measurements: the quality of the returned solution and the number of evaluations used to find it. A lower final error alone does not establish that a method reached the target earlier.

We ask whether screening candidates can reduce the number of exact evaluations needed to reach a fixed target. In a small illustrative experiment, screening reaches the target in fewer evaluations across three paired runs. These observations motivate a larger comparison under a common stopping rule.
